# The 0 to 15k profit runner

Item 3 of every START A MARKET brief, and the thing Jack needs before he can
answer a field sales owner who asks what it costs.

## Priced two ways, always both

- **A monthly recurring figure.**
- **The whole thing paid upfront at 20 percent off** the total of the monthly
  run.

Both, every time, on the same page. Never one without the other. The upfront
discount is 20 percent, it is not a negotiating range and it does not move
because a prospect pushes.

## The shape

Zero to fifteen thousand. Not a package, a runner: stages that go in order,
each one earning the right to the next.

For each stage:

- **What it is**, in a line the owner would use himself.
- **What has to be true before it starts.** Preconditions, not aspirations. If
  the previous stage did not produce its number, the next one does not begin.
- **What it is worth**, traced to a figure that came out of the prospect's own
  answers, not a benchmark.
- **What kills it.** Named, specific and honest. Every stage has one.

"What kills it" is the part that gets skipped and it is the part that makes the
whole thing believable. A runner with no failure mode reads as a pitch.

## Where the numbers come from

**Off his own answers, through `rep-burn-live`.** The Rep Burn Map already puts
four numbers on the board and every one of them traces to something the owner
said out loud:

```
burn     the reps who never got past the ramp
slow     the half of the ramp you could have skipped
gapReal  the gap to his best rep, counted at a third, capped
nnValue  the not now pile, at five in a hundred
```

The runner is priced against what it puts back, and the map already says what
each month puts back. **It can never claim to put back more than was lost.**
That ceiling is in the maths on purpose, keep it.

If a figure in the runner cannot be traced to one of his answers, take it out.

## Never inflate

Jack's own book argues for the honesty cap and the mean 0.1 multiplier. A runner
that promises the full gap rather than a third of it is off voice, and it is
also how you lose a man who knows his own trade better than you do.

Where a number is a guess, call it a guess in the line it appears.

## What is not settled, and must not be invented

**The actual euro figures are not written down anywhere a phone session can
read.** They are on the Mac, and `assessment-business/offer-and-pricing.md`
still records trades as the lead niche from a decision on 19 July, which
contradicts the field sales pivot of 29 Aug and has not been corrected.

So:

- **Do not invent a monthly figure and present it as his price.** If Jack asks
  for the runner and the pricing is not readable, build the stages, the
  preconditions and the kill conditions, leave the figures as blanks tied to the
  prospect's own numbers, and tell him plainly that the price is the one thing
  you cannot source.
- When he names a figure, use it and remember it for the rest of the session.

Per his own rule, the newer decision wins and the older file gets corrected.
Correcting `offer-and-pricing.md` needs the brain repo, see
`docs/brain-repo-setup.md`.

## The measuring stick

Zero strangers have ever paid Jack. A runner is not the product, a stranger
paying is.

So finish by saying which stranger this is for and what the next move is to put
it in front of him. **One action at the end, not three.**
