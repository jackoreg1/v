Read this first

A leak is money you already did the work to earn, going to someone else or going nowhere. The call that rang the next plumber. The quote that died in your head on a Friday night. The invoice you never sent. None of it needs new customers. It is your money, already in motion, falling out of the bucket.

The one rule this audit runs on

Your numbers, not mine. Every figure you write in here comes out of your own normal week. Not your best week. Nothing in this book is invented and nothing is a promise, every number is an estimate built from what you put in. It is a torch, it shows you where to look.

What you need

  • Your phone and about 20 minutes.
  • Your bank app open for one of the pages.
  • Honesty about how last week actually went.

The three levers

Every fix in here pulls at least one of three levers: it makes you money, it saves you time, or it lifts the quality of the job. If a fix someone is selling you pulls none of them, you do not need it.

Two honesty rules, before you start.
1. When you total your leaks at the end, cap the total at 15 percent of your yearly turnover. Big numbers feel great and do nothing. The cap keeps you honest.
2. A leak under a few grand a year is real, but it is not your priority. You will fix the biggest one first, not the easiest.

LEAK 1The missed call

Ask yourself

What happens when someone rings and I can’t answer?

You know you have it if you hear yourself saying

“It goes to voicemail.” “I ring back when I’m off the ladder.” “I’d miss a few a week.”

The maths

Calls missed in a week  x  0.3  x  average job €  x  48
The 0.3 is honest: roughly 3 in 10 missed callers would have booked. The rest were never going to. Example: miss 4 a week on €300 jobs: 4 x 0.3 x 300 x 48 = €17,280 a year ringing the next name on the list.
Your number  € a year

First move

Missed call text back. The second a call goes unanswered, a text fires by itself: “Sorry I missed you, on a job, tell me what you need and I’ll come straight back.” It catches the lead before they dial the next lad. The bigger version is a receptionist that answers and books for you, that is a proper setup job.

Effort: the text version needs a hand to set up right. The habit version, texting back yourself within five minutes, is free and starts today.

The maths

Quotes a month you reckon died of slowness  x  average job €  x  12
A lost quote is a whole job. Even one a month at €400 is €4,800 a year. First to quote usually wins the job, everyone in the trade knows it.

First move

Templates. Take your last good quote, strip the customer’s details out, and that is now your template. Every new quote is fill in the blanks, out the same evening from the phone. Five minutes, not forty. The bigger version turns a voice note from the van into a tidy quote before you’ve left the driveway.

LEAK 4Nothing gets followed up

Ask yourself

Where do jobs and customers fall through the cracks?

You know you have it if

“Once a quote’s out I forget about it.” “Half never hear back from me.” “I’ve a phone full of old customers I never ring.”

The maths

Quotes sitting out there with no answer  x  0.1  x  average job €
The 0.1 is deliberately mean: even if only one in ten came back off a single follow up, that is free money for one text. And separately, the old customers in your phone are gold sitting idle. One message pulls jobs this week, for nothing.
Your number  € waiting on one text

First move

Tonight, chase your three newest unanswered quotes, word for word: “Hi [name], following up on the quote from [day], will I pencil you in for next week?” Then once a season, the reactivation text to old customers: “Jack here, any jobs need doing before the winter? I’ve a slot free next week.” Change the name, obviously.

Effort: free. Costs one thumb.

LEAK 5Admin eating the evenings

Ask yourself

What am I doing at the kitchen table at ten at night that should be done inside working hours?

You know you have it if

“The books on a Sunday night.” “Paperwork after the tea, every night.” “I didn’t start a business to do this.”

The maths

Evening and weekend hours a week on paperwork  x  €25  x  48
€25 an hour is the honest office rate for the admin itself. If those hours are also costing you jobs you never quoted, price them at your trade rate instead, €45 or whatever yours is, and watch the number jump.
Your number  € a year

First move

Name the single biggest drain. Not “admin”, one job, the books or the quotes or the receipts. Then cut its steps in half before you buy anything: what can be done once instead of weekly, on the phone instead of at the table, by a photo instead of typing. Automate last, not first. The real cost of this leak was never the €25 an hour anyway, it is the reason you started the business slipping away.

Effort: naming it is free. The fix depends on the drain.

Ask yourself

How much of my day goes on answering the same stuff?

Hours a week answering messages  x  €25  x  48

Saved replies. The five messages you type most, price ranges, availability, area covered, “on a job, back to you at six”, become saved templates on your phone. One evening to set up, years of thumb saved. The bigger version answers the common stuff for you before it ever reaches your pocket.

LEAK 8The diary in your head

Ask yourself

Walk through last Monday. Any double bookings, forgotten jobs, or driving the county in the wrong order?

You know you have it if

“It’s all in my head.” “A notebook in the van.” “I’ve double booked before, aye.”

The maths

Jobs missed, double booked or messed in a month  x  average job €  x  12
Count the angry phone calls too. A missed slot is a lost job today and a customer who doesn’t ring next time, that second cost isn’t in the formula but you know it’s real.
Your number  € a year

First move

One shared calendar, on the phone, and every job goes in it the minute it is agreed, standing in the driveway if needs be. No second system, no notebook backup, one place. The bigger version is a booking link, customers pick a slot themselves and phone tag dies.

How do new customers actually find me? What comes up when I google my trade and my town?

“Word of mouth only.” “I don’t ask for reviews.” “I know I should sort Google, I never did.”

The maths, and an honest one

Your Google reviews   vs   the fella ranking first
This is the one leak you can’t honestly put a euro on from the kitchen table, so this book won’t pretend to. Count the gap instead. Review count is a known local ranking factor. More reviews means ranking higher means free inbound leads, and it compounds while you sleep.
The gap   reviews behind

First move

Text your last five happy customers the direct review link tonight, two taps for them. Then make it a habit, the link goes out the day after every job, every job, forever. And claim your Google Business Profile properly, photos, hours, area, it is free and half your competitors never bothered.

LEAK 10The same five questions all day

Ask yourself

What do I answer fifty times a week? For most trades it is price, availability, and do you cover my area.

You know you have it if

“I answer the same five questions every single day.”

The maths

Already counted in Leak 7’s hours. Do not count it twice.
This page is here because the fix is different. Leak 7 is about the volume of messages, this one is about the same five answers, and those you can answer once, in public, forever.

First move

Write the five answers once, properly, and put them where people actually look before they message you: your Google Business Profile, your socials, a pinned reply on WhatsApp. Every question answered in public is a message that never arrives. The bigger version is a system trained on your own answers that handles the lot and only hands you the real enquiries.
