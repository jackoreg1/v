Teardown 01

The empty machine and the full one.

Five weeks inside one coaching account. In July it was built and empty. In August it was full, and the money was still falling out the same holes.

One real account, anonymised. Every number below was read off the live screen on the date beside it, not remembered and not estimated.

What this is

An online coaching business. One coach, sells a programme at a bit under a thousand euro, gets his leads through Instagram and a lead magnet funnel. The kind of setup thousands of people have.

I got into the account properly on 20 July and I have been reading it since. What follows is what I actually found, in order, including the bits where I was wrong and the bits where nothing I did made any difference.

The point of a teardown is not to show you a clean win. It is to show you what the inside of a real account looks like when somebody finally counts it.

20 July

The machine was built. Nothing had gone through it.

The first read was of the plumbing, because that is what everybody assumes is broken. It was not broken. Landing page live and clean. Thank you page live, the PDF downloaded. Booking calendar live with real slots. Lead capture firing, tagging correctly, source populating on its own.

Then I opened the pipeline.

7opportunities in the whole pipeline. Five of them were test cards nobody had cleaned up.

One real qualified lead. Zero booked. Zero showed. Zero closed. Every value sitting at nought euro. Not one lead had ever reached a booked call inside the system. Not once, since it was built.

Weeks of work went into a machine, and the account proved almost nothing had ever passed through it.

The instinct at that point is to build more. Add reminders. Add a qualification form. Add another automation. That is the trap, and I wrote it down at the time so I could not talk myself out of it later: the constraint is not more machine. The constraint is that nothing is going in, and the one lead that qualified is not being walked to a call.

So the top fix was not a build. It was to force one real lead the whole way through the machine that already existed, and watch whether the money actually logged. You cannot trust a system you have never seen complete a lap.

25 July

Then I read the inbox instead of the CRM.

The pipeline said seven people. The inbox said something else entirely.

639conversations walked, one at a time
106people who had messaged in and never been replied to

Two of them were warmer than anything on the official lead list. They were not lost, they were not unqualified, and nobody had done anything wrong on purpose. They had messaged, and then a day went by, and then a week.

That is the first real lesson in this whole teardown. The CRM is where you record what you decided to do. The inbox is where the business actually is.

24 August

A month on, and I still could not prove a single euro.

By late August the top of the funnel had genuinely improved. Calls were happening. Real conversations, real people, real interest. So I went to check the money and I could not.

The payments view, the calendar and the opportunities all came back refusing the read. Not empty, refused. Which is its own lesson, because the honest answer that day was "unknown, not zero", and I wrote exactly that rather than guessing in either direction.

What I could read was the conversations. Two hundred and seventy two unread. And in among them, the one that mattered: a lad who had written, on the 23rd at 20:25, that he was ready to go and how does he pay. He gave his phone number at 20:54. Then nothing. The conversation moved somewhere I could not see and no receipt ever appeared.

What I refused to write that day

The CRM had him tagged as won. I did not record him as won, because a stage in a CRM is a thing somebody clicked. It is not money. The only thing that proves money is a receipt, and there was not one.

27 August

Fifty six conversations, read properly, and there it was.

This is the day the number came out. Every conversation from the previous seven days, all fifty six of them, full message history on each. Not a dashboard summary. The actual words.

Three deals had gone quiet with nobody pushing them. Not lost, not rejected. Quiet.

That is the sentence I keep coming back to. Not a broken integration. Not a bad ad. A man put his hand up, asked what it costs, and five days later still did not know. There is no automation that fixes that, and there was no automation causing it either.

Two calls that happened and were never recorded

Separately, two real sales calls had been held that week with no value logged against them anywhere. Which means the pipeline total was understating the business to itself. If you only create the record when you remember to, the number on your dashboard is a mood, not a measurement.

And most of the inbox was not leads at all

Of the fifty six threads, about twenty were one line engagement. Thanks for the follow. A reel share. Nice one. Real people, no intent, no action needed. Another handful were agencies cold pitching and outright spam.

That matters because an inbox with 274 unread on it feels like an emergency, and roughly half of it is not work. You cannot start until you have sorted it into piles.

28 August, tonight

Where it actually stands.

The unread count is 274. It went up, not down.

The price question from the 22nd got answered this evening at 18:59. Six days. He has not replied yet, and that is fair enough.

As of the last check tonight there is still no verified payment, no confirmed booked call and no signed agreement I can put my hand on. The last campaign that went out reached nineteen people, all nineteen delivered, two opened it, nobody clicked.

The honest bit

I have not fixed this yet.

Five weeks in, the counting works and the money has not moved. If I finished this teardown with a win I would be making it up. The three stalled threads are still there. What changed is that they are now on a list with a number beside them, and before I did the read nobody in the business knew they existed.

That is not nothing, but it is not a result either, and I am not going to dress it up as one.

The four things I would take out of it

  1. Count it before you fix it. Every instinct says build. Every read says the build was already fine and empty. You are not allowed a solution until you have a number.
  2. A stage is not money. A receipt is money. Won in a CRM means somebody clicked won. If you cannot point at a transaction, write unknown, and go and ask.
  3. Most of the inbox is not leads. Sort it into four piles before you touch anything. Real deals, slow burners, engagement, spam. Half the panic disappears in ten minutes.
  4. The leak is almost never the machine. It is days going by. Nobody decided to let a lad wait six days for a price. It just happened, because nothing in the business was counting days.

Do this on your own account this week

You do not need any of my tools for this. An hour and a notepad will do it.

  1. Open your inbox and go back thirty days. Not your CRM. Your actual messages. Every thread, oldest first.
  2. Mark every thread where they spoke last. That is the whole exercise. If the last message in the thread came from them, it goes on the list.
  3. Put a euro figure beside each one. What would that person be worth if they bought. Not a forecast, just the price of the thing you sell.
  4. Add up the ones that asked a direct question and never got an answer. Price, availability, how do I pay. That subtotal is the number that will make you sick, and it is the one worth acting on first.
  5. Answer three of them today. Not all of them. Three. The ones with the biggest number and the most days on them.

What good looks like

If your list comes back empty, brilliant, you are already tighter than the account in this teardown and you can stop reading. If it comes back with four figures on it, that is normal, and it is the cheapest money you will find this month because you already paid to get those people in.
