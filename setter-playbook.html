Who to book

Your pay depends on Conor closing. Book the right people and he closes more. Book everyone and he stops trusting your bookings.

The person Conor helps most

  • A lad in his 20s to mid 40s. Women are welcome too, same rules.
  • Trains, plays sport, runs, or did until his body stopped him.
  • Desk job, or lots of sitting and driving. Has a wage.
  • Pain or restriction: lower back, a disc, hips, hamstrings, groin, knees. Or just stiff and stuck.
  • Has tried physio, stretching or YouTube routines. Still breaking down.
  • Wants something specific back: the gym, 5 a side, GAA, a race, Hyrox, lifting the kids without his back going.

The DM flow

7 stages, in order. Most bookings die because someone jumped from Open straight to Book.

  1. Open

    Start the chat. Mention why they're there. One easy question.

    Did the hip routine come through grand? Is it more tightness you're working on, or have you a bit of pain going on?

  2. Dig

    Find the problem. Where, how long, what it's stopping.

    How long has the back been at you?

  3. Tried

    What they've already done: physio, stretching, rest. This is the gap Conor fills.

    Did the physio help at all, or does it keep coming back?

  4. Feel

    Why it matters, and what happens if nothing changes.

    If it's still like this in 6 months, what does that look like for you?

  5. Want

    The goal, made specific. Not "be healthy". "Play 5 a side on Thursdays."

    What would you love to be able to do again?

  6. Fit

    Check the boxes you haven't ticked yet: commitment, timing, money.

    If there was a plan that sorted this, could you give it 3 or 4 sessions a week?

  7. Book

    Offer the free movement assessment. Give 2 real times, not "whenever suits".

    Conor does a free movement assessment where he looks at exactly what's going on and maps out a plan. Would Tuesday at 7 or Wednesday at 12 suit better?

Then lock it in: confirm, send the matching proof, and remind them the morning of and an hour before. Scripts for all of it are in the next tab.

Rules of a good DM

  • Short. 1 to 3 lines, like texting a mate.
  • One question per message.
  • Use their words. If they say "my back keeps going", you say "keeps going".
  • Ask, don't pitch. The more they talk, the more they book.
  • No booking offer until you know the problem and why it matters.
  • Reply fast. A START DM answered in 5 minutes books far more often than one answered tomorrow.
  • Voice notes build trust fast. Use them once you're chatting.
  • Never argue. Never chase someone who said no. Mark it and move on.
  • Never give health advice. See Health line.
  • Log the chat in the tracker before you close the app.

Objections

An objection is usually a question in disguise. Stay calm, find out what's behind it, answer that one thing.

How much is it?

Usually: interested, but braced for a big number.

Fair question. It depends on online or in person and what you need, Conor maps that out on the free assessment. I'll be straight with you, it's proper 1 to 1, so it's an investment. If it sorts the [back] for good, is that something you'd be open to?

If Conor OKs a ballpark, give it. Never make one up.

I'll think about it.

Usually: something's unclear, or it isn't a priority yet.

Course. What's the main thing you're weighing up?

Then answer that one thing. Nothing else.

I can't afford it.

Usually: either true, or they don't see the value yet.

Totally get it. Is it that money's tight right now, or more that you're not sure it'd work for you?

If money's tight, point them to the free content or a workshop and mark Nurture. Don't push.

I'll just do the free routine for now.

Usually: not convinced they need help yet.

Do, it's a great start. Give it a couple of weeks and let me know how the [back] feels. If it's still at you, the assessment is there.

Set a follow up for 14 days.

I've tried physio, nothing works.

Usually: lost hope. This is Conor's sweet spot.

That's the story with most of the lads Conor works with. Colin was the same, physio told him weak glutes, weak hamstrings, and it kept coming back. Want me to send you his story?

What if this makes my back worse?

Usually: scared. Someone asked exactly this under his BACK reel.

Fair worry. That's why Conor starts with an assessment, he looks at what's going on before he gives you anything. Most people who come to him have been through physio already. Has a physio or doctor had a look at it?

If they mention any red flag, go to Health line.

I don't have time.

Usually: picturing 2 hours a day in the gym.

How many times are you training at the minute? Conor builds it around what you've got, most of his clients train 3 or 4 times a week.

I need to ask my partner.

Usually: a shared money decision. Respect it.

Makes sense. Would it help if they jumped on the assessment too? Then you both hear the plan.

Is it online or in person?

Usually: a buying question. Good sign.

Both. In person around [Dublin and Meath], or fully online with a programme built for you. Which would suit you better?

Check Conor's in person locations with him first.

I'm already with a PT.

Usually: either happy, or quietly not.

Ah sound. Is it going well, or what made you check out Conor's stuff?

If they're happy, wish them well and leave it.

Maybe in the new year.

Usually: a real timing issue, or a soft no.

No bother. What would need to happen for it to be the right time?

Set a follow up for the date they give you.

Is this Conor?

Usually: just checking. Always tell the truth.

Health line

You're not a physio, and a DM isn't an assessment. This tab protects the lead, Conor, and you.

Never

  • Diagnose. ("Sounds like sciatica.")
  • Promise a fix. ("Conor will sort your disc.")
  • Give exercises for their pain.
  • Tell them to stop seeing their physio or doctor.
  • Make up results or timelines.

Say this instead

Conor works with a lot of people with [back] stuff. He'll look at exactly what's going on at the assessment.

Stop selling if you hear these

Tell them to go to A&E today:

  • Numbness around the groin, bum or inner thighs.
  • Losing control of their bladder or bowels, or they can't pee.
  • Weakness or numbness in both legs, or weakness that's getting worse.

Thanks for telling me. Please don't sit on that one, numbness there or changes with your bladder need to be checked today, and A&E is the right place for it. Look after yourself, and let me know how you get on.

Tell them to see a GP before training:

  • Pain that started after a fall, crash or accident.
  • Fever, or losing weight without trying.
  • Pain that's worse at night and won't ease.
  • A history of cancer, or recent surgery.

Thanks for telling me that. Honestly, that's something to get checked by a GP first, just to be safe. Once you're cleared, Conor would love to help you build back up. Will you let me know how you get on?

Either way: mark them Nurture in the tracker and tell Conor.

Other numbness or tingling

Plenty of Conor's clients had it. Niall used to go numb sitting in the car. Don't judge it yourself. Ask if a doctor or physio has looked at it, and put it in Conor's handoff note. Conor decides, not you.

Numbers

5 numbers tell you everything. The tracker works them out for you.

The 5 numbers

  1. Reply rate. Replies ÷ openers sent. Aim for 30 to 50% on warm leads.
  2. Booking rate. Booked ÷ replies. Aim for 15 to 25%.
  3. Show rate. Turned up ÷ booked. Aim for 70% or better.
  4. Close rate. Bought ÷ turned up. Conor's number, but your bookings drive it. Aim for 25 to 40%.
  5. Speed to lead. How fast you answer a START DM. Aim for under 15 minutes during your blocks.

Common starting targets, not laws. After a month, your own numbers become the targets.

The deal

Sort this before you send a single DM. It decides what every hour of your work is worth.

10 things to agree with Conor

  1. What am I paid on? Booked, showed, or closed? Best for you: a fee for every qualified call that shows, plus a % of every sale.
  2. What %, of what? A % of cash collected is normal. If a client pays monthly, do you get your % on every payment?
  3. Renewals. If a client you booked renews or upgrades, are you paid on that? Colin's been with Conor 12 months. That's where the money is.
  4. Which leads are mine? Anyone you message first? Website bookings? Get it in writing, even a WhatsApp.
  5. Payday. Weekly or monthly, and how will you see what closed? Ask to see sales for your leads, or share the tracker.
  6. Access. Inbox access through Meta Business Suite, so he doesn't have to share his password. Plus his booking link.
  7. You or him? Do you message as "Jack from Conor's team" or as Conor? As yourself is more honest, and you can send voice notes.
  8. Price talk. Can you give a ballpark in DMs? What exactly is the €1,200: how long, online or in person?
  9. Other coaches. Are you free to set for coaches who aren't in his space?
  10. Review. Look at the numbers together after 30 days.

What's normal

Ranges people commonly quote, not rules. Every deal is different.

% only

Often 5 to 10% of cash collected. On €1,200 that's €60 to €120 a sale, and slow weeks pay nothing.

Per show

A flat fee for each qualified call that turns up. Pays for your work, not for Conor's closing.

Hybrid

A smaller fee per show plus a %. The best fit for a €1,200 offer.

What I'd ask for: €25 for every qualified call that shows, plus 10% of all cash collected from clients you booked, including monthly payments and renewals for 12 months. Review after 30 days.

How setters get to full time

  1. Get great with Conor. 30 to 60 days. Your numbers become your CV.
  2. Get proof. Once your bookings close, ask Conor for a testimonial and referrals.

WHO TO BOOK: THE 6 POINT CHECK
1 Problem Conor fixes (pain, injury, stiffness, plateau)
2 It matters (stopping or limiting something they care about)
3 Why now (an event, sick of the cycle, getting worse)
4 Will do the work (3 to 4 sessions a week, online or in person)
5 Open to investing (gets that 1 to 1 coaching costs money)
6 Their call (adult, own money)
5 or 6: book. 3 or 4: keep talking and find the missing box. 0 to 2: give value, mark Nurture.
Only tick a box if the chat shows it. Never guess.
Don't book: under 18, only wants freebies, health red flags, can't train 3 times a week, says plainly there's no money, wants a 2 week fix.

THE DM FLOW
Open, Dig (problem, how long), Tried (physio, stretching, rest), Feel (why it matters, what if nothing changes), Want (a specific goal), Fit (check missing boxes, pre frame the investment), Book (free movement assessment, offer 2 specific times), Lock in (confirm, send matching proof, remind the morning of and 1 hour before).

HOW TO WRITE MY MESSAGES
- 1 to 3 short lines, like texting a mate. Irish and casual: "grand", "sound", "no bother" are fine. One emoji at most, like 🤝 or 💪.
- One question per message. Mirror their words.
- Ask, don't pitch. No booking offer until I know the problem and why it matters.
- Write as Jack from Conor's team, with Conor in the third person. If I say "as Conor", write as Conor in the first person.
- No em dashes. No hyphens used as dashes. Use commas or full stops.
- Never invent results, prices, dates, availability or scarcity. If a fact isn't in this prompt or in what I give you, put [brackets] for me to fill in.
- Don't quote a price unless I tell you Conor allows it. Use: "it's proper 1 to 1, so it's an investment, not a €20 app."
- No pressure, no fake urgency, no guilt trips. If they say no, be gracious and point them to Conor's free content.

HEALTH LINE (NEVER BREAK THIS)
- Never diagnose, never promise to fix an injury, never give exercises for their pain, never tell them to stop seeing a physio or doctor.
- Numbness around the groin, bum or inner thighs, losing control of bladder or bowels, can't pee, or weakness in both legs or weakness getting worse: kindly tell them to go to A&E today, drop the sales chat, and tell me to flag it to Conor.
- Pain after a fall or accident, fever, weight loss without trying, pain worse at night that won't ease, history of cancer, recent surgery: tell them to see a GP first, mark Nurture, and tell me to flag it to Conor.
- Any other numbness or tingling: ask if a doctor or physio has looked at it, and put it in the handoff note. Conor decides, not us.
